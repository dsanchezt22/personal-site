# Landing and navigation

An off-white paper page. The hero is a full-bleed, hand-wobbled line drawing of a circuit board (key-switch footprints, a controller, a memory mesh, an adder driving a "29" display, a 48 V stage) with routed copper between the parts. The board leaves an empty area on the left where the copy sits: "Stanford EE student · Class of 2029", the name, "I design circuits and build software.", and framed links for GitHub, X, LinkedIn, Email, and Resume (icons only when the five labels would not fit on one row). There is no intro, WebGL scene, motion toggle, or sound.

The board is grey ink; a soft spot of colour follows the pointer across it, and a few blue pulses travel along traces. With reduced motion the pulses are hidden and nothing else animates.

The first screen must stay smooth. The board is painted once: the pulses and the colour spot are separate layers that change only by transform or opacity, so the wobble filter never re-runs (docs/adr/006-paint-once.md). Check it with `perf/measure.sh <url> <outdir>` from this skill: in the `idle-*` rows raster is 0, in the `pointer-*` rows raster is near 0 and fps is 60, and no row has a long task. A pulse must stay on its copper trace at every viewport size, and the colour spot must stay registered with the board (the copper under the pointer turns gold exactly where the grey copper is).

The browser tab shows a small processor with "dst" on it (lavender body, pins above and below at tab size, pins all round at 48px and up, and on a paper tile for the home-screen icon). It is generated by `npm run icons`; the letters must be legible at 16px on both a light and a dark tab.

The sticky header links to #about (background), #work (projects), #hobbies, and #contact; at 320px all four still fit on one row beside the monogram. Section titles are single lowercase words with one objective description line beneath, not slogans; each section opens with a rule drawn from schematic symbols. Skip to content focuses main. Without JavaScript all section content is readable; project details live in dialogs and need JavaScript.

Check 1440x900, 1024x768, 390x844, and 320x568: document width equals viewport width, the hero copy stays inside the board's empty area (no part or trace under the text), and console errors are absent.
