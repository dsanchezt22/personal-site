Research

Typing-force energy harvester

NC Science & Engineering Fair · 2024 · North Carolina School of Science and Mathematics

Can the force of typing on a mechanical keyboard switch be harvested as usable energy? The rig: a piezo disc under an MX black switch on a force plate, a four-diode bridge rectifier charging a 470 µF capacitor, and a rocker switch that dumps the stored charge into a red LED.

I swept press force (2.5 ± 1 N) and disc diameter (10 and 41 mm) over 360 actuations, about a minute of typing, logging force and voltage with Vernier probes in Logger Pro. A press of about 3 N produced a spike of about 6 V on the 41 mm disc. Voltage rose with force and with disc size; at low force the 41 mm disc reached about 2 V and the 10 mm disc never did.

Conclusion: intermediate-to-large piezos under normal typing force capture usable energy, a low-cost, battery-free idea for low-resource settings.

RMX-1000-style effects box

Stanford EE 102A final project · with Anthony Yang and Daniel Tauhert · in progress

A DIY version of the Pioneer RMX-1000: a small box with a button pad and knobs that takes music in and applies effects in real time. Echo and delay, reverb, filter sweeps (low-, high-, and band-pass, plus a bass/mid/treble isolator), pitch changes, and build-ups before a drop.

Signal chain: music in → ADC → a microcontroller running the effects from the pad and knobs → DAC → speakers. Each effect is prototyped first in Python and MATLAB (echo as an LTI system, h[n] = δ[n] + a·δ[n−D]; reverb as convolution with a room impulse response; FFTs and spectrograms), then moved to hardware: an ESP32, a Raspberry Pi, or an audio board in a project-box enclosure, laser-cut if time allows.

Status: being built now.

AI

Realization Engine

Spatial Realization · Stanford CS146S

Realization Engine turns a property listing's photos into an explorable, editable 3D model. You can view it as a dollhouse, walk through it, or see the floor plan, and furnish it by describing a piece ("a 2.1 m navy sofa against the wall"). Pieces are placed at real size and checked for clearance and fit: doors unblocked, tight passages, refused placements.

Room sizes are inferred from the photos, and the app says so: the model is an estimate, not a survey.
